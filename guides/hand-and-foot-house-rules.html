Hand and Foot House Rules: The Ones to Agree On First

By Will McCullough, DigiRune Studios · November 19, 2026

Short answer: there is no official rulebook for Hand and Foot, so every table plays some house rules. The rules that differ most between the published versions are how much of the discard pile you can take, how many cards you are dealt, what you need on the table before you can go out, how many wild cards a meld can hold, and what red threes are worth. Agree on those five before the first deal, and write them on the score sheet.

Hand and Foot is a Canasta-style game played with several decks, where each player gets two stacks of cards: a hand to play first and a foot to pick up once the hand is gone. The card game rules site pagat.com says there are "numerous variations of this game and no standard rules." That is why a game learned at one family table can feel wrong at another.

A common baseline

The most usual version on pagat.com, contributed by Bill Whitnack, is a good place to start. Here it is in brief:

  • Four players in two partnerships, five decks with jokers (270 cards).
  • Each player gets a hand of 13 cards and a foot of 13 cards.
  • On your turn, draw two cards from the stock, or take the top seven cards of the discard pile if you hold a pair that matches the top card and meld them at once.
  • Melds run from three to seven cards. Twos and jokers are wild, but a meld needs at least twice as many real cards as wild cards.
  • The first meld each deal must be worth at least 50, 90, 120 and then 150 points over the four rounds.
  • To go out, your team needs two clean piles, two dirty piles and one wild pile, your partner must have picked up and played from their foot, and you must ask your partner's permission.
  • Piles of seven score 500 clean, 300 dirty and 1,500 wild. Going out is worth 100. Red threes are 100 each if laid down, minus 100 if not.

The house rules that differ most

1. Taking the discard pile

In the baseline you can take at most the top seven cards. Other tables let you take the whole pile, which is how the Saskatchewan version on pagat.com plays. Taking the whole pile can mean a much bigger pickup, so it can change how long you wait before grabbing it.

2. How many cards you are dealt

Thirteen and thirteen is the baseline. The Saskatchewan version deals 11 for the hand and 13 for the foot, and Steve Simpson's version deals 11 and 11. Fewer cards in the hand means you reach your foot sooner.

3. What you need to go out

The published versions disagree here too. The baseline asks for two clean, two dirty and one wild pile. The Saskatchewan version needs one of each. Steve Simpson's version, played without partners, needs only one clean and one dirty pile, plus a final discard.

4. Wild cards in a meld

The baseline allows one wild card in a meld of three to five and two in a meld of six or seven. Other groups only ask that real cards outnumber wild cards, so a dirty pile can carry three wilds. Decide whether your table plays wild piles at all, and what they score.

5. Red threes

In the baseline they are plus 100 on the table and minus 100 if you are caught holding one. In Steve Simpson's version a red three can never score for you: you discard it as soon as you can, and any red three still in your hand or foot at the end of the hand costs 500 points. A reader on the Denexa Games blog noticed exactly this: some written rules have no penalty for unplayed red threes and others take off 500. The answer there was sensible: house rules are fine, as long as everyone knows and agrees to them up front, ideally in writing.

Also worth a quick word

  • Pile bonuses. Steve Simpson's version scores a clean pile at 300 and a dirty pile at 100 instead of 500 and 300.
  • First meld minimums. The baseline does not count red threes or pile bonuses toward them, and the Saskatchewan version asks for a hidden canasta, seven cards built in your hand, to open in the fourth round.
  • How many rounds make a game. The baseline is four deals.

Write it down before you deal

Spend two minutes before the first hand and write your answers at the top of the score sheet:

  • Discard pile: top seven, or the whole pile?
  • Cards dealt: 13 and 13, 11 and 13, or 11 and 11?
  • To go out: which piles, and must you ask your partner?
  • Wild cards: how many per meld, and do wild piles count?
  • Red threes: plus 100 down and minus 100 held, minus 500 if caught holding one, or no penalty?
  • Pile bonuses and first meld minimums for each round.
